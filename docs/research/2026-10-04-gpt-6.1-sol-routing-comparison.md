# GPT-6.1 Sol for implementation and research

Research date: 2026-10-04. This is a recommendation, not a routing change or a local benchmark result.

Subsequent decision: the user approved migrating repository templates for `general` to GPT-6.1 Sol `medium`, and `implement-complex` and `architect` to GPT-6.1 Sol `high`. The baseline and statements about unchanged routing below describe the research snapshot before that decision. Installed configuration has not been migrated and no local model trial has been run. The researcher binding remains GPT-6 Sol `medium`.

## Question and current baseline

Should GPT-6.1 Sol replace GPT-5.6 Sol for complex implementation, given the concern that GPT-6 Sol was closer to GPT-5.6 Terra?

The current templates bind `implement-complex` to `openai/gpt-5.6-sol`, `high` (`skills/setup-opencode-routing/templates/agents/implement-complex.md:4–5`) and `researcher` to `openai/gpt-6-sol`, `medium` (`templates/agents/researcher.md:4–5` in the same skill). Templates do not prove active runtime bindings. No controlled evidence for the remembered GPT-6 Sol/Terra equivalence was found in the local material or primary sources reviewed.

## Recommendation

GPT-6.1 Sol is a stronger replacement candidate than the previous documentation alone established for GPT-6 Sol. The benchmark follow-up below strengthens this to a preferred migration candidate, subject to a bounded local trial and provider compatibility. Do not yet claim that it reliably beats GPT-5.6 Sol on subtle implementation or research accuracy at our exact effort settings: broad benchmark improvements are not a direct test of our roles.

Start with GPT-6.1 Sol `high` versus GPT-5.6 Sol `high` for `implement-complex`, and GPT-6.1 Sol `medium` versus GPT-6 Sol `medium` for `researcher`. Keeping effort labels fixed isolates the configuration change, not equal compute: effort is model-specific. Preserve role permissions, prompts, verification contracts, and review stages. Change defaults only after confirmed provider access/compatibility, useful outcome evidence, and user approval.

GPT-6.1 Sol has half the input/output rates of GPT-5.6 Sol and one-quarter its cached-input rate. Against GPT-6 Sol, input/output rates are unchanged and cached input is halved. GPT-5.6 Sol's listed prices are promotional, available at least through 2026-11-21.

Cache writes are separately charged (GPT-6.1 Sol: $2.50/M). Long requests above 272K input tokens incur full-request premiums: GPT-6.1 Sol explicitly lists 2x input/cache rates and 1.5x output. Processing tiers, tool calls, regional premiums, generated reasoning, retries, and intervention affect total cost. Token prices alone cannot establish cost per accepted implementation. GitHub's announcement says provider list pricing applies under usage-based billing; actual account billing was not inspected.

## Compatibility and omitted-exception checks

The [GPT-6 migration guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model), especially “GPT-6.1 Sol,” “Limitations,” and “Update API and model parameters,” was checked separately from headline positioning:

- GPT-6.1 Sol supports `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, `max`; not `none` or `minimal`.
- Tool calling requires Responses. Chat Completions is supported without tools. Confirm the selected OpenCode provider adapter, not just its model catalog, supports this path.
- The guide advises preserving current effective effort where supported. Astra-specific behavior and benchmark claims elsewhere on that page must not be attributed to Sol.
- GPT-6.1 Sol's model page says Fast mode is unavailable with EU residency.
- All four model pages list the same 1,050,000 context window, 922,000 maximum input, and 128,000 maximum output; this does not show equal effective long-context accuracy.
- GitHub's announcement says GA with gradual rollout to Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise, subject to administrator controls. GA does not prove this account's access or an exact OpenCode Copilot model ID.

## Suggested bounded comparison

Use identical starting revisions, prompts, tools, acceptance criteria, and permissions for each pair. Include a realistic multi-file change, a subtle bug with hidden edge cases, and a review task with known defects. For research, include conflicting-version specifications and a source table whose headers/qualifiers change the interpretation.

Score accepted correctness, serious missed findings, unsupported claims, instruction/scope adherence, retries, human intervention, wall-clock time, and total billed usage. Repeat enough cases/runs to avoid treating one success as a stable ranking. Keep a fallback if quality regresses. This describes a future trial; no paid calls or benchmark runs were performed.

## Benchmark follow-up: LLM Stats and Artificial Analysis

Checked 2026-10-04. The coordinator opened the comparison pages, methodology pages, Artificial Analysis article, medium-effort model page, and OpenAI release cited below. These findings supersede the initial evidence gap about independent benchmark comparisons, but not the gap about our exact workflow.

### LLM Stats: distinguish coverage-adjusted ratings from shared results

The [requested comparison](https://llm-stats.com/models/compare/gpt-5.6-sol-vs-gpt-6.1-sol) reports:

| LLM Stats index | GPT-5.6 Sol | GPT-6.1 Sol |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Overall | 54.1 (45 evals) | 52.0 (9 evals) |
| Reasoning | 53.0 (29 evals) | 42.2 (3 evals) |
| Coding | 44.9 (17 evals) | 38.4 (2 evals) |
| Agents | 40.4 (26 evals) | 38.1 (4 evals) |

These are conservative TrueSkill ratings, not success percentages on an identical test set. The site's [methodology v3.1](https://llm-stats.com/methodology/llm-stats-score) says missing results are not failures; incomplete coverage retains greater uncertainty. Lower ratings with thinner coverage must not be explained entirely as uncertainty, but neither do they establish that 6.1 Sol is worse on matched coding tasks. The exact contribution of coverage to each rating is not exposed in the retrieved methodology.

The comparison says 6.1 Sol wins all seven shared results: DeepSWE 1.1, GDP.pdf, HealthBench, HealthBench Consensus, HealthBench Hard, HealthBench Professional, OSWorld 2.0. That is one software-engineering result, one document-reasoning result, four related medical results, and one computer-use result—not seven independent coding wins. Raw score rows and exact effort/harness provenance were not available in the retrieved comparison text, so treat this as an aggregator-reported tally rather than a fully audited head-to-head evaluation. The page also reports 9 index evaluations but 10 individual benchmark results for 6.1 Sol; those counts should not be silently equated.

The site's 5.6 Sol pricing ($5/$30 input/output) differs from the current official model card ($4/$20 promotional). Its advertised 2.8x blended token saving is therefore not the current official-price comparison. Use the official pricing table above, not the aggregator's price ratio.

The [Terra versus 6 Sol comparison](https://llm-stats.com/models/compare/gpt-5.6-terra-vs-gpt-6-sol) gives overall ratings of 50.1 versus 49.0, coding ratings of 41.2 versus 35.5, and 45 versus 5 overall evaluations. Its four shared results favor 6 Sol on three (Agents' Last Exam, FrontierCode 1.1, OSWorld 2.0) and Terra on DeepSWE 1.1. This makes the user's earlier concern understandable from this site's ratings; it does not establish universal equivalence. The original basis of the earlier decision is still unknown.

### Artificial Analysis: more relevant independent evidence

Artificial Analysis conducts its own evaluations, so its [September 29 release analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-6-1-sol-replaces-gpt-6-sol-after-just-7-days-with-near-astra-intelligence) is a primary report by an independent benchmark operator, rather than model-vendor marketing. It reports:

- Intelligence Index: 6.1 Sol gains **5 points over 5.6 Sol** and **4 over 6 Sol**, landing one point below Astra.
- At max effort, Intelligence Index cost per task is **$0.72** for 6.1 Sol versus **$1.99** for 5.6 Sol and **$1.05** for 6 Sol. These are costs of that evaluation suite, not expected costs of our implementation tickets.
- Coding Agent Index: 6.1 Sol max gains **3 points over 6 Sol max**. The xhigh variant gains **6 points over 6 Sol max**, scoring one point above Astra in that comparison.
- **6.1 Sol xhigh outperformed its max variant by 3 points** in the Coding Agent Index. This argues against assuming that maximum effort is automatically best; it does not establish that xhigh is necessary or optimal in our harness.
- The Intelligence Index report says 6.1 Sol used **10–30% more output tokens than 6 Sol** across effort settings. This qualifies GitHub's earlier fewer-token report: different workloads and harnesses can yield different token-efficiency results.
- The report describes an 8-point AA-Omniscience accuracy improvement and a hallucination-rate reduction from 60% to 54% versus 6 Sol at max effort. These are benchmark-specific rates, not typical user-facing error rates or evidence about medium-effort research.

The [Coding Agent Index v1.5 methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/coding-agents-benchmarking) combines DeepSWE v1.1 (113 tasks), Terminal-Bench 4.0 (66), and SWE-Atlas-QnA (124), equally weighting the three benchmark scores. It averages three attempt scores per task, then across tasks; this is pass@1 averaging, not best-of-three success. DeepSWE uses isolated verification and restricted internet; repository Q&A uses an LLM judge. Critically, each public row represents an **agent variant, not just a model**. The article alone does not establish identical OpenCode harnesses or our high-effort setting. The coding delta quoted here is against 6 Sol, not a direct numeric delta against 5.6 Sol.

For the researcher role, the [6.1 Sol medium model page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gpt-6-1-sol-medium) reports Intelligence Index **48**, at **$0.21 per index task**. Its index includes document reasoning, long-context reasoning, knowledge, science, coding, and agentic work. This is useful broad medium-effort evidence, but no isolated medium-versus-medium source-reconciliation result was verified. Some charts were unavailable in the text retrieval; do not invent their missing values.

### OpenAI's benchmark report: supporting but vendor-reported

The [6.1 Sol release](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-sol/) reports matching Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 and exceeding 6 Sol's best score by **6.4 percentage points**, at lower effort/cost. It also reports more than doubling 6 Sol's Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 score at max effort. The latter tests scientific workflows with code and terminal tools, not our read-only researcher role. The retrieved prose does not expose all raw score/effort chart rows. OpenAI explicitly cautions that research/API environments differ in prompts, tools, and effort from production products.

### Updated judgment

Independent broad-intelligence and coding-agent evidence now supports preferring 6.1 Sol as the migration candidate, rather than relying only on near-Astra positioning. LLM Stats' lower composite coding rating is a reason to examine coverage and matched tests, not a sufficient reason to keep 5.6 Sol. Retain the proposed high-effort implementation and medium-effort researcher comparison before deployment. Consider a separately authorized xhigh coding comparison only if the high trial leaves a relevant quality gap; do not raise effort automatically. No configuration edits or paid evaluations were performed.
